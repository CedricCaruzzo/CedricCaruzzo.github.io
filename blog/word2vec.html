The Geometry of Words

A Word2Vec playground built from scratch in PyTorch

← Back to Projects & Writing Hub

How Much Fits in 300 Numbers?

A word embedding represents a word as a dense vector. If “unique” meant perfectly orthogonal, a 300-dimensional space could hold only 300 unique directions. A real vocabulary has tens of thousands of words, so useful embeddings cannot depend on strict orthogonality.

High-dimensional space can fit a huge number of nearly orthogonal vectors. This project explores that geometry by implementing Word2Vec's Skip-Gram with Negative Sampling model from scratch.

Learning from Context

Skip-Gram learns to predict surrounding words from a target word. Given fox, for example, it learns a vector that helps predict nearby words such as quick, brown, jumps, and over.

Negative Sampling

A softmax over the full vocabulary is expensive. Negative sampling turns the task into binary classification: distinguish real (target, context) pairs from randomly generated ones. This requires far less computation.

The Limit of Static Embeddings

A static embedding gives bank one vector whether it means a river bank or a financial institution. It cannot move closer to river or money based on the sentence.

Contextual models such as Transformers solve this by computing a new representation for each occurrence of a word.
